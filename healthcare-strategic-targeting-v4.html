01 · Executive Thesis

Healthcare systems aren't maintenance prospects. They're operational complexity at scale.

The strongest opportunity areas in healthcare facilities operations sit upstream of work orders — in compliance defensibility, capital project commissioning, multi-site standardization, and the operational governance required to run distributed clinical environments without operational drift.

01

Environment of Care

EOC programs cross Facilities, Safety, EVS, and Clinical Engineering. Most systems lack a single operational source of truth, creating audit risk under Joint Commission and DNV survey cycles.

02

Joint Commission / DNV Readiness

Compliance defensibility is increasingly evidence-based. Systems that can produce documented work history, PM completion, and asset compliance status on demand outperform on survey outcomes.

03

HTM / Clinical Engineering

Clinical engineering operations are converging with facilities. Unified asset visibility across both biomed and facilities equipment is becoming a board-level operational requirement.

04

Asset Lifecycle Governance

Aging infrastructure across acute-care systems is creating capital pressure. Systems need defensible lifecycle data to prioritize replacement, defer responsibly, and protect the capital budget.

05

Capital Planning

Major construction, renovation, and acquisition activity creates commissioning, asset onboarding, and operational handoff complexity that legacy systems were not built to handle.

06

Space Management

Clinical space utilization, infection prevention zoning, and renovation churn require dynamic space governance. Static floor plans are no longer sufficient.

07

Multi-Site Standardization

Merged, acquired, and expanded systems inherit fragmented operational stacks. Standardization across campuses is one of the highest-leverage operational initiatives a system can run.

08

Work Order Governance

Volume isn't the problem. Governance is. Routing, prioritization, vendor coordination, and completion verification across distributed environments drives operational signal quality.

09

Deferred Maintenance Visibility

Most systems can't quantify their deferred maintenance liability with operational confidence. That gap creates audit, safety, and capital allocation risk.

10

Compliance Defensibility

Operational evidence — not policy — is what stands up under survey. Systems need an operational record that holds up to CMS, Joint Commission, DNV, and state regulators simultaneously.

02 · Why This Approach

Reframing the territory around operational signal, not bed count.

Bed-count tiering is the right starting filter. It is not, on its own, the right qualification framework. This approach layers strategic operational triggers on top of the existing target list to improve conversation relevance and executive alignment.

Current Default

Volume-driven outbound

  • Bed count as primary qualifier
  • Generic facility management pitch
  • Sequence-led, not signal-led
  • Conversations land at coordinator level
  • Pipeline measured by activity, not quality
  • Lists treated as the input, not the strategy
This Framework

Signal-driven targeting

  • Operational complexity layered onto bed count
  • Trigger-event sequencing (M&A, capital, survey, leadership)
  • Account-specific operational point of view
  • Executive-level language, executive-level access
  • Conversation quality as the leading indicator
  • Lists as raw material, strategy as the multiplier
Goal: Improve conversation relevance, strategic alignment, and meeting quality — not replace activity. Dial volume and discipline still matter. What changes is what we say when someone picks up.

04 · Campaign Sequence

A five-phase outbound architecture built for executive-level access.

Each phase has a single objective. The point isn't to "touch" an account — it's to earn each next conversation by saying something operationally specific that the prior message couldn't have said.

01

Strategic Familiarity

Objective · Earn name recognition without asking for anything.
"Saw the Bold Forward expansion news from Mayo this week — interesting commissioning challenge across a project of that scale. Not pitching anything. Just wanted to put a name to your inbox in case our path crosses around facility operational governance down the road."
ChannelLinkedIn + email
CTANone — name recognition only
Cadence1 touch, low pressure
02

Problem Identification

Objective · Surface a real operational tension specific to their environment.
"Quick observation — most systems coming out of a merger spend the first 18 months reconciling work order governance across two operational stacks. Curious whether that's been on Froedtert ThedaCare's radar yet, or if integration sequencing has pushed it out further."
ChannelEmail + dial
CTALight question, not a meeting
Cadence2–3 days after touch 1
03

Operational Reframe

Objective · Reframe their current state through a strategic operational lens.
"Most systems treat CMMS as a work order tool. The ones doing this well treat it as their operational evidence layer — the system of record that holds up under Joint Commission survey, capital planning review, and merger due diligence. Worth a 15-minute conversation about how that's framed at the executive level?"
ChannelLinkedIn message + voicemail
CTA15-minute conversation
Cadence5–7 days after touch 2
04

Executive Discovery

Objective · Earn a discovery conversation framed at the operational governance level.
"The reason I'm reaching out is specific — not generic. Your system is in active expansion and we're seeing systems at that scale hit operational governance friction within 12 months of new capacity coming online. I'd like to understand how you're thinking about commissioning, asset lifecycle, and survey defensibility going into FY26."
ChannelDirect dial + email
CTA30-minute executive discovery
Cadence7 days after touch 3
05

Business Case Alignment

Objective · Translate the operational conversation into a measurable business outcome.
"Quick recap on where this fits — three operational outcomes I'd anchor this conversation around: defensible compliance evidence, capital planning visibility, and multi-site standardization. If any one of those is a FY priority on your side, that's the right place for us to start."
ChannelEmail recap + scheduled follow-up
CTAWorking session w/ ops leader
CadencePost-discovery, within 5 days

06 · Persona Map

Eight executive personas. One operating language.

Each persona owns a different slice of the operational picture. The unifying language is the same — operational governance, defensibility, capital alignment — but the framing shifts based on what they're measured on.

VP / SVP Facilities
Owns enterprise facilities strategy
Cares About
Operational consistency across the system, defensible compliance posture, capital plan credibility with the CFO and board.
Likely Frustration
No single operational view across markets. Spends too much time aggregating data manually to answer executive questions.
Strategic Language
"Operational governance," "system of record," "defensibility," "standardization at scale."
KPIs
PM compliance %, survey readiness score, capital plan execution, deferred maintenance trend.
Director Facilities Operations
Owns day-to-day operational execution
Cares About
Work order throughput, vendor coordination, technician productivity, completing the PM schedule on time.
Likely Frustration
Tooling that creates more documentation work than it removes. Reactive culture, no time to get ahead.
Strategic Language
"Workflow governance," "vendor accountability," "PM completion confidence," "operational signal."
KPIs
WO completion time, PM compliance, backlog trend, vendor SLA performance.
HTM Director
Owns clinical equipment operational integrity
Cares About
Clinical equipment uptime, patient safety, regulatory compliance for medical devices, alarm management.
Likely Frustration
HTM treated as a cost center. Separate from facilities ops, which limits operational visibility and cross-functional governance.
Strategic Language
"Clinical equipment lifecycle," "biomed and facilities convergence," "alarm and event governance."
KPIs
Equipment uptime, PM completion, recall response time, work order resolution by clinical area.
Clinical Engineering Leader
Owns biomed program and integration
Cares About
Integrating clinical equipment data with the broader operational picture, supporting clinical staff with reliable equipment.
Likely Frustration
Operating with limited data infrastructure. Equipment records often live in legacy systems disconnected from the rest of operations.
Strategic Language
"Integrated operational record," "clinical-facilities alignment," "evidence-based equipment governance."
KPIs
Mean time to repair, equipment availability, recall closure, PM adherence.
EOC / Safety Officer
Owns Environment of Care program
Cares About
Joint Commission and DNV survey readiness, environment of care chapter compliance, defensible documentation.
Likely Frustration
EOC evidence is fragmented across facilities, safety, security, and EVS. Surveys reveal gaps that operational tools didn't expose in advance.
Strategic Language
"Survey defensibility," "EOC chapter evidence," "rounding governance," "performance improvement loop."
KPIs
Survey scores, RFI count, EOC rounding completion, corrective action closure time.
Capital Planning Leader
Owns multi-year capital investment
Cares About
Defensible prioritization of capital projects, accurate deferred maintenance liability, asset lifecycle data quality.
Likely Frustration
Capital decisions made on incomplete operational data. Hard to defend prioritization to the CFO without underlying lifecycle visibility.
Strategic Language
"Lifecycle defensibility," "capital prioritization framework," "operational data as capital input."
KPIs
Capital plan execution, deferred maintenance trend, asset condition index, project ROI.
Support Services Leader
Owns non-clinical operational services
Cares About
Coordination across EVS, transport, food service, security, and facilities. Patient experience as an operational outcome.
Likely Frustration
Cross-functional operations live in different tools. Hard to coordinate response and accountability across service lines.
Strategic Language
"Service line coordination," "operational handoff governance," "patient experience as an operational metric."
KPIs
Service response time, patient satisfaction scores, cross-functional SLA adherence.
Chief Operating Officer
Owns enterprise operational outcomes
Cares About
Operational consistency across the system, margin protection, defensibility under regulatory and board scrutiny.
Likely Frustration
Operational fragmentation across markets, no single operational truth, limited ability to standardize without imposing top-down friction.
Strategic Language
"Enterprise operational governance," "operational coherence," "defensibility," "scale without fragmentation."
KPIs
Operating margin, system-level KPI consistency, regulatory standing, capital deployment efficiency.
07 · Why Now

Market timing favors operational governance conversations.

Seven concurrent pressures are pushing operational governance from a facilities-level concern to an executive-level conversation. Each one is a wedge into a more strategic dialogue.

▲ Pressure 01

Staffing Strain

Facilities and HTM staffing shortages are forcing systems to do more with less, raising the value of automation, evidence, and clean handoffs.

▲ Pressure 02

Deferred Maintenance

Pandemic-era capital deferrals have created a defendable-but-quantified backlog that boards are starting to ask about explicitly.

▲ Pressure 03

Merger Integration

Multiple regional mergers in 2023–2025 (Froedtert ThedaCare, Emplify, Endeavor) are entering peak operational integration windows.

▲ Pressure 04

Operational Fragmentation

Health systems are running 6–12 disconnected operational tools per market. Consolidation is no longer a nice-to-have at the executive level.

▲ Pressure 05

Compliance Pressure

Joint Commission, DNV, and CMS scrutiny is increasingly evidence-based. Documentation of operational rigor matters more than policy on paper.

▲ Pressure 06

Aging Infrastructure

The median age of acute-care facilities is rising. Asset lifecycle data becomes a board-level conversation, not a maintenance department line item.

▲ Pressure 07

Capital Constraints

Margin pressure is forcing capital prioritization to be more defensible — every approved project has to displace another credible candidate.

08 · Recommended Next Steps

A six-step playbook to operationalize this framework.

Sequenced for the next 30–60 days. Each step is independently valuable — even if only the first two are adopted, the targeting quality improves materially.

STEP 01Prioritize Tier-1 systems

Confirm the 24 Tier-1 accounts as the strategic core. Apply daily dial focus against P0 and P1 systems first. Treat Tier-2 as a secondary cadence, not a parallel one.

STEP 02Map stakeholders

For each Tier-1 account, build the 8-persona map: VP Facilities, Director, HTM, Clinical Engineering, EOC, Capital Planning, Support Services, COO. Sales Navigator is sufficient for the first pass.

STEP 03Align trigger events

For each account, identify the current operational trigger (merger, capital project, leadership change, survey cycle). Refresh weekly. The trigger is the reason for the call.

STEP 04Launch executive messaging campaign

Run the five-phase sequence against the top 10 accounts first. Track conversation quality (executive engagement, depth of dialogue) as the leading indicator, not contact count.

STEP 05Build operational POVs

For each Tier-1 account, develop a 1-page operational point of view that captures the trigger, the operational hypothesis, and the executive question. Use as the asset that opens the discovery conversation.

STEP 06Measure conversation quality vs. volume

Add a "conversation quality" measure alongside dial count: did we reach the executive level, did we surface a real operational topic, did we earn a next step. Volume still matters — but it's no longer the only signal.

This initiative was designed to improve targeting precision, strategic relevance, and executive alignment across healthcare outreach efforts.

It's not meant to replace activity — dial volume, discipline, and trade-show energy still drive the engine. It's meant to make every conversation count for more by giving the territory a strategic spine.

Open to feedback on where this could be sharpened, restructured, or refined further. The strongest version of this framework will be the one shaped by the team using it.
